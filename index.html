How the placements were made

The three spectrums come from Zoheir Esmail's post, which draws on Shaykh Dr Vinay Khetia's framing. The scholars' positions are approximate readings of what each is best known for in their published work and in the secondary literature; the post itself places no one. Every placement is contestable, since most scholars hold mixed positions and changed over a lifetime. A position says nothing about piety or correctness.

Part 2's axes are inspired by the 12Axes political test and adapted to questions Shia communities argue about. The society and politics compass places a wider cast than part 1: jurists and maraji', thinkers and academics, political and community leaders, and preachers. Each is placed only where their public positions are documented, and those placements are rougher than part 1's: they read public stances on politics, social life and philosophy, which few figures set out systematically. Where a figure has no documented view on philosophy, their dot is hollow and sits at the middle of that axis.

Your answers are stored only in your own browser on this device (its local storage), so the test resumes where you left off and your results are there when you come back. When you finish a part, your answers and scores are also recorded anonymously for research, as the notice before the test says (not for anyone who says they're under 18). A result code for each part lets you carry your answers to another device yourself. "Clear my data" on the insights page removes everything, including your recorded result.

The test is a self-reflection tool, not a validated measure. The reading, activity and belief suggestions on the insights page describe tendencies among people with similar views, not predictions about you.
